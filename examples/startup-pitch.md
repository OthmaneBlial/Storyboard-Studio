## Research teams lose the thread between interviews
A fictional product team stores interview notes in documents, decisions in chat, and research plans in spreadsheets. Insights become difficult to trace when the project changes hands.
Notes: Entirely synthetic business example. This deck contains no real customer results, market estimates or fundraising claims.

## The problem is continuity, not another note-taking tool
### Fragmented evidence
An insight has no durable link to the interview excerpt that supports it.
### Unclear decisions
The team cannot easily tell which open questions changed the product direction.
### Expensive handoffs
The next researcher starts by reconstructing the previous project.

## Evidence to gather before expanding the pilot
### Interview researchers
Observe how participants locate an excerpt and reconstruct a decision. Treat findings as unresolved until the author reviews them.
### Test the handoff
Ask a second researcher to continue a synthetic project without a verbal briefing.

## Two options trade speed against traceability
### Improve existing documents
Add templates and naming conventions. Faster to start, with continued manual linking.
### Build a focused research workspace
Link excerpts, questions and decisions locally. More initial engineering, with a narrower handoff workflow.

## Recommend a focused six-week pilot
The proposed workspace keeps source excerpts next to the decisions they inform. Pilot one handoff workflow before investing in integrations or automated analysis.

## The main risk is adding work for researchers
Keep capture lightweight. Do not make authors maintain a second copy of their notes. Review the time spent linking evidence alongside any improvement in handoff clarity.
